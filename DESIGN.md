# Research Smarter — Design

## Motivation

In 2024, the American Psychological Association said in an article that, when it comes to research, "Ideally, AI tools will progress to the point that they can enable researchers to focus on the more complex and cognitively demanding elements of scholarship." 

Research Smarter is a skill that acts as a research assistant that works with the user to refine their research topic, stress-test their arguments, and do the mental work of engaging with the research process. The goal is for the skill to develop the user's critical thinking skills as AI becomes more prevalent as a research tool. 

## Guiding Principles

- The skill is a tool for getting the user to do the critical thinking
  themselves. It doesn't tell the user what to do or feed them hints.
- Its framework is **questions**. It doesn't tell the user they're wrong; it
  asks tough questions that make them think and engage with the material.
- It doesn't simply agree with the user. It's encouraged to disagree, to play
  devil's advocate, and to ask questions unprompted.
- The skill will include disclaimers at the beginning of each phase, stating
  that AI models can sometimes hallucinate, and to encourage the user to do
  their own research and fact-check what the model produces.

## Phase 1: Refine the research question or topic

1. **Preliminary briefing.** The skill does some preliminary research on the
   topic and gives a quick briefing.
2. **Patterns.** It identifies patterns across existing texts and literature.
3. **Mind map.** It presents a mind map of possible avenues the user could
   explore. The user then can explore and choose a direction. 
4. **Refining questions.** Once the user chooses a direction, the skill follows
   up with refining questions, guided by a framework for what makes a good
   research question:
   - It flags topics that are oversaturated, or angles that don't add anything
     new.
   - It brainstorms alternate framings and suggests different angles to
     explore.

**Outcome:** The user has come up with a specific direction for their research.

## Phase 2: Second briefing and devil's advocate

1. **Targeted research.** The skill does more research on the angle the user
   picked and gives a second briefing.
2. **Back-and-forth.** The user says, based on this research, where they want
   to go and what they want their arguments to be. The skill:
   - plays devil's advocate and shows what the other side might argue
   - points out logical fallacies in the user's arguments
   - pushes back on and helps refine the user's thinking
   - challenges assumptions and asks questions unprompted

The skill leans toward disagreement, always phrased as questions.

**Outcome:** The user has solidified the direction of their research.

## Phase 3: Plan the research

1. **Sub-questions.** The skill prompts the user to break their research
   question into smaller sub-questions.
2. **Where to look.** For each sub-question, the user decides where the answer
   might be found: what kinds of catalogs or platforms, possible keywords, and
   who might hold the expertise.
3. **Critical thinking moment.** The user considers what kinds of research
   could support their argument and what kinds could counter or complicate it.

The first two steps help guide the skill's research. The main purpose of this
phase, especially the last step, is to prepare the user to know what they're looking for, what's useful, and what they want to do with the research the skill will conduct in Phase 4, so the user can parse that information more strategically and take ownership of the research.

**Outcome:** The user has a refined research question, and understands what they're looking for in the research phase. 

## Phase 4: Research

The skill does the research and delivers:

- the relevant sources it compiled
- a summary of what it found and what it didn't find
- additional questions or fine-tuning
- suggested avenues to explore, including things the user should look into
  more on their own

**Outcome:** The user has a long database of relevant research.

## Phase 5: Post-research supplement

After the user has done the research, both with the skill and on their own, the
skill helps them develop, refine, and stress-test their ideas. For example, it
can test how an argument holds up against different arguments or perspectives,
to strengthen everything that comes after the research process.

**Outcome:** The user develops strong, stress-tested arguments that are well thought-out and supported by research.
