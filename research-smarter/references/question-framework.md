# What makes a good research question

> **Provisional.** The project's own research question frameworks are still
> being written. Until they replace this file, use these general criteria.

Use these criteria to ask questions about a user's research question, never to
rewrite it. When a criterion isn't met, ask a question that helps the user see
it, for example "What would count as an answer to this?"

A strong research question is:

- **Focused.** Narrow enough to answer well within the assignment's length
  and deadline. Watch for big, vague terms ("effects", "society", "work").
- **Arguable or open.** Thoughtful people could reach different answers. A
  question with a single factual answer ("When did France ban phones in
  schools?") is a fact to look up, not a research question.
- **Answerable with available evidence.** The user can realistically find
  sources or data to address it.
- **Adds something.** It isn't a heavily studied question restated. If the
  angle is crowded, the user can say what new angle, population, place, time,
  or method they bring.
- **Significant.** The user can say why the answer matters, and to whom.
- **Clear.** Key terms are defined or definable, and someone else reading it
  would understand what's being asked.
- **Fits the research type.** For example, an empirical study needs something
  measurable; a historical project needs available sources from the period.
