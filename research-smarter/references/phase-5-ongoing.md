# Phase 5 · Ongoing stress testing (optional)

**Goal:** a resource the user can come back to at any time, after their
question is refined and their research is done, to keep testing their
thinking as they work.

Phase 5 is not a required step, and the user doesn't need to bring in all
their research. They bring in one thing at a time:

- an argument or claim they're making
- a source they're relying on
- a line of reasoning they're unsure about

If they arrive with a research log, follow the resume steps in SKILL.md first.
If they arrive without one, ask briefly what their research question and
position are, in their own words, so you can test the piece against them.

## How to stress-test it

Use the lens for their research type (`research-types.md`). Depending on what
they bring:

- **Credibility (for a source).** First ask for the user's own assessment:
  who wrote it, where it was published, how they know what they claim, when,
  and any possible bias. Then share yours, using the checklist in
  `sources.md`. Check that the source exists; if you can't verify it, say so.
- **Logical fallacies (for an argument).** Ask questions that lead the user
  to any gaps in reasoning. Name the fallacy only if they don't find it.
- **Evidence and perspectives.** Phase 2 tested reasoning before the evidence;
  this phase tests arguments against the evidence and other viewpoints. For
  example: "This source seems to contradict your claim. How do you account
  for that?"
- **Critical thinking.** Ask probing questions that help the user strengthen
  their own thinking. Acknowledge what's strong.

As always: correct facts directly, question reasoning, never write their
content, one or two questions per turn.

## Wrapping up

There's no fixed exit check. When the user is done with a piece, ask them to
say in a sentence what they'll change or keep, then offer to update their
research log with what they tested.

**Outcome:** whatever the user brought in has been stress-tested, and they
leave with questions to keep strengthening their work.

## Example

> **Phase 5 · Ongoing stress testing (optional)**
>
> Welcome back. Last time you finished your research plan and sorted your
> sources. Before we start, can you restate your thesis in your own words?
>
> *(after the user restates it and shares a news article)*
>
> Before I weigh in: how much would you trust this article, and why? Think
> about who ran the survey it reports on and how they chose who to ask.
