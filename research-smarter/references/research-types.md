# Research types

The phases stay the same for every kind of research. What changes is the
central thing being tested (the user's **working position**) and the lens you
use to challenge it in Phases 2 and 5. Intake (Phase 0) sets the type.

| Research type | Working position | What to challenge in Phases 2 and 5 |
|---|---|---|
| Argumentative essay | Thesis | Counterarguments, logical fallacies |
| Empirical study | Hypothesis | Other explanations, confounding factors, whether it can be disproved, sample size and method |
| Literature review | How the review organizes the field | What's left out, which voices dominate, whether the gaps are real |
| Historical or interpretive | Interpretation | Other readings, bias in the sources, anachronism (judging the past by today's assumptions) |
| Exploratory, no position yet | The question itself | Assumptions built into the question, scope, whether it can be answered |

## Using the lens

- **Argumentative essay.** Ask what a thoughtful opponent would say, and how
  the user would answer. Look for overgeneralization, cherry-picked examples,
  and causal leaps.
- **Empirical study.** Ask what else could explain the expected result, what
  result would prove the hypothesis wrong, and whether the planned method and
  sample can actually answer the question.
- **Literature review.** Ask how the user decided what's in and out, whose
  perspectives are missing, and whether a "gap" is really unstudied or just
  hard to search for.
- **Historical or interpretive.** Ask how someone from another tradition or
  time would read the same sources, who produced the sources and why, and
  whether present-day assumptions are creeping in.
- **Exploratory.** There's no position yet, so test the question: what does it
  assume, is it answerable with available evidence, and is the scope right
  for the assignment?

If a project doesn't fit any row, pick the closest one and adapt; say which
lens you're using so the user understands the kind of questions coming.
