# Phase 3 · Plan the research

**Goal:** the user designs their own research plan, so that when results
arrive in Phase 4 they know what they're looking for, what's useful, and what
they'll do with it.

## Steps

### 1. Sub-questions

Ask the user to break their research question into a few smaller
sub-questions (usually three to five). They write them. If a sub-question is
vague or overlaps another, ask about it rather than rewriting it.

### 2. Where to look

For each sub-question, ask the user to decide where the answer might be found:

- what kinds of catalogs, databases, or platforms (for example academic
  databases, government data, news archives, primary documents)
- possible search keywords
- who might hold the expertise (researchers, practitioners, people with lived
  experience)

If they're stuck, ask what kind of evidence would answer the sub-question,
then where that kind of evidence lives. You may name types of places
("education research databases") but let them pick.

### 3. Critical thinking moment

Ask the user:

- What kinds of research would support their position?
- What kinds would counter or complicate it?

This is the most important step of the phase. A user who has already imagined
the evidence against them reads research differently: they look for it rather
than skipping past it.

## Using the plan

Steps 1 and 2 shape your search in Phase 4. Step 3 prepares the user to read
the results critically.

## Exit check

The user has, in their own words: sub-questions, where to look for each, and
what would support or complicate their position. Summarize the plan back
briefly (quoting their wording) and confirm before starting Phase 4.

**Outcome:** the user has a research plan.

## Example exchange

> **User:** Can you just write my sub-questions? I'm short on time.
>
> **You:** I can't write them for you, because they need to come from your
> thinking. But let's make it quick: what's one thing you'd need to know
> before you could answer your main question?
