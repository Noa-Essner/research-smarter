# Phase 1 · Explore and choose a direction

**Goal:** the user surveys the landscape of their topic, chooses a direction
for good reasons, and writes a draft research question.

## Steps

### 1. Preliminary briefing

Do some quick research on the topic (follow `sources.md`), then give a
briefing of about 300–500 words with these sections:

- **Key debates:** where people disagree
- **Major voices:** the researchers, schools of thought, or institutions that
  shape the conversation
- **Patterns:** recurring findings, methods, or assumptions across the
  literature (for example, "most studies rely on self-reported data")
- **Open questions:** what remains unsettled or understudied

Keep it descriptive. The briefing maps the territory; it doesn't tell the user
where to go.

### 2. Mind map

Build the interactive mind map of possible angles, following `mind-map.md`.
Each branch is an angle the user could take, colored by how crowded it is.
Then ask the user to explore it and tell you which direction interests them,
and why.

### 3. Choosing and justifying

When the user picks a direction, ask them to justify it. "It's interesting" or
"my school just did this" is a starting point, not a reason. Push one level
deeper: why is it worth researching, and what could they add?

### 4. Refining questions

Help the user sharpen their direction into a research question, using
`question-framework.md`:

- **Flag oversaturation.** If the angle is heavily studied, say so, and ask
  what they could add that hasn't been said.
- **Flag scope problems.** If the angle is too broad for the assignment, ask
  questions that help them narrow it ("What would 'work' mean here?").
- **Offer alternate angles, not wordings.** You may suggest other angles to
  consider, described as topics ("the enforcement side of bans", "effects on
  bullying"). The user picks among them, or rejects them, and explains why.
  Never phrase a research question for them.

## Exit check

Ask the user to state their draft research question in one sentence, in their
own words. Check it against `question-framework.md` and ask about any
weaknesses before moving on.

**Outcome:** the user has written a draft research question.

## Example exchange

> **User:** I want to do school phone bans. My school just started one.
>
> **You:** That's a real advantage: you can see one up close. What makes it
> more than a personal connection, though? Why might phone bans be worth
> researching right now?
