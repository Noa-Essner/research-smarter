I'm going to describe an idea for what I want to build in this repository. There's a quote from the American Psychological Association that says, ideally, AI tools will progress to the point that they can enable researchers to focus on the more complex and cognitively demanding elements of scholarship. I want to build a skill that'll be a research assistant for students or anyone who wants to do research. But the goal would be not that the skill does the research for the student. The idea would be like, okay, maybe students aren't doing all the Googling, the deep dives themselves. That's what the AI model is doing. But the AI model is working with the student to help make sure the student is engaged with the process and help build the student's critical thinking skills throughout the process. So for phase one, the student wants to research something. The skill will first, phase one, help them refine their research question or topic. So they'll do some the skill will do some preliminary research on the topic to give a quick briefing. It'll identify some patterns across existing texts and literature. Then it'll give a mind map of possible avenues for the user to explore new ideas. And once the user selects, okay, where specifically they want to go, then the skill will sort of follow up with refining questions. So there'll be a framework to know what a good research question is. So the skill will, it'll flag topics that are oversaturated or angles that don't add anything new. The skill will also brainstorm alternate framings or suggest different angles for the user to explore, and end this phase with the user having come up with a refined research question. the key is that the skill isn't telling the user what to do or feeding them hints, it's acting like a tool to get the user to do all the critical thinking themselves. Next is phase two, where the skill, after the user refines their research question, will do a little bit more research on the specific angle the user selects, and then sort of give them a second briefing, and then the skill will act like a research assistant. So this is where the skill really like plays devil's advocate, shows what the other side might argue, shows logical fallacies in your arguments. It'll be this back and forth stage where the user talks about, based on this research, where they want to go, what they want their arguments to be. The skill will push back, refine. The idea is that the skill won't simply agree with what the user says. If anything, it's going to trend more towards playing devil's advocate, towards asking questions unprompted, to challenging the user's thinking, its assumptions, their arguments, really making sure the user is asking questions and really engaging and really thinking deeply and critically about their topic. The model is encouraged to disagree. The skill is encouraged to trend toward disagreement. And again, the framework for this skill is question. So the skill won't tell the user that they're wrong. It'll ask lots of tough questions and get the user to think and really engage with the material. The third stage or phase is refining the research question and next steps. So the skill will then prompt the human to break their research question into smaller sub-questions. Then it will prompt the user for each sub-question to decide where the model might search for the answer, like what types of catalogs or platforms, potential keywords, who might hold the expertise. And then for a critical thinking moment, the prompt will ask the user what types of research could support this argument and what type could counter-complicate it. This whole phase, phase three, isn't so much to guide Claude's research, maybe the first two parts, but especially the last one about the critical thinking part. It's more to have the user be prepared to engage with the process so that when Claude gives them all the research they've compiled in the next phase, the user will be prepared to actually parse that information and understand, what am I looking for? What's useful? What am I thinking? What am I actually looking forward to doing with this? So that they're actually engaging with the process, they're understanding how to parse it, and they have a really clear sense of direction so that they can take ownership over the research. Phase four is where Claude actually does the research. The skill does the research. It compiles the relevant information sources, a summary of what the skill found or didn't found, additional questions or some fine tuning. It might give the user suggestions for potential avenues to explore, suggestions of what the user should look into more themselves. And then the last part would be a post-research supplement. So the main part of this skill is getting the user to really refine what they want to look for, really refine their arguments, refine their thinking, refine their questions, prepare to do the research. Once the user's done the research, both with Claude and then on their own, then the skill can help the user sort of develop, refine, or stress test their ideas, really, like test, for example, how an argument holds up against different arguments or perspectives, just to really strengthen everything that comes after the research process.
